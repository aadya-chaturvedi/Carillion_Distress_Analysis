# Carillion Financial Distress Analysis (2014–2016)

## Overview

This project explores the financial performance of Carillion plc, a UK construction and services company that collapsed in January 2018.

As a Computer Science student interested in developing my analytical skills, I wanted to explore how financial data could be used to identify warning signs of a company experiencing financial difficulties.

Using Carillion's published annual reports from 2014 to 2016, I built an Excel workbook to calculate financial ratios, visualise trends and apply the Altman Z''-Score model.

## Tools and Methods

- **Microsoft Excel:** Data collection, calculations and visualisation.
- **Financial ratios:** Liquidity, profitability, leverage, interest cover and cash flow.
- **Altman Z''-Score:** A four-factor financial distress model adapted for non-manufacturing companies.

All financial figures were collected from Carillion's published consolidated financial statements.

## Key Findings

**1. Revenue growth did not translate into stronger profitability.**

Revenue increased by approximately 25.8% between 2014 and 2016, while profit from operations declined by approximately 9.1%.

**2. Cash generation weakened.**

Operating cash flow fell from £123.8 million in 2014 to £73.3 million in 2016, despite revenue increasing.

**3. Average borrowing increased.**

Average net borrowing increased from £450.7 million in 2014 to £586.5 million in 2016. Year-end borrowing was consistently lower than average borrowing, highlighting the importance of analysing more than one debt measure.

**4. The Altman Z''-Score indicated financial vulnerability.**

The calculated scores were approximately 0.97 in 2014, 1.15 in 2015 and 0.63 in 2016, suggesting a deterioration in financial health before Carillion's collapse.

## Conclusion

This project helped me understand how financial statements can be analysed using simple calculations and data visualisation.

The findings suggest that several financial warning signs were visible in Carillion's published accounts before its collapse, particularly weakening profitability, poor cash conversion and increasing borrowing.

The Altman Z''-Score was used as an indicator of financial distress rather than a definitive prediction of bankruptcy.

## Limitations

The analysis uses historical annual accounts and simplified financial ratios. It does not account for every factor behind Carillion's collapse, including subsequent events, contractual risks and management decisions.
